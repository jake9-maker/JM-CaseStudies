## In 60 seconds

- **The problem:** Two appraisal products, Max and ClearCar, were run by separate teams. Features were built twice, bugs fixed twice, and dealers had to learn two interfaces for the same job.
- **What I did:** I led the audit, the research, and the stakeholder alignment, designed and prototyped the merged experience, and presented the business case to executive leadership.
- **The result:** Leadership unanimously approved the consolidation. I'm leading its phased rollout: phase one launched in June 2026, with the rest following through 2027.

## The problem

Everyone assumed Max and ClearCar served different use cases, so the default was to keep both.

The research showed they were nearly identical, with mostly cosmetic differences. Separate teams with separate incentives meant neither was optimized for dealers. As one dealer put it: "Why do I have to learn two different interfaces for the same thing?"

**The insight:** don't default to keeping both. Audit rigorously, keep what's genuinely unique, and consolidate the rest.

![Combined Appraisal Jobs to Be Done Audit](/images/combined-appraisal-experience-jobs-to-be-done-audit.png)

## Three decisions that drove the outcome

### 1. Replace opinion with evidence

"They look similar" wasn't going to convince anyone. I audited both products side by side (jobs to be done, personas, and user journeys) to measure exactly what overlapped.

**Result:** The 80/20 split came straight from the data. Even the teams that initially resisted agreed the findings were rigorous.

![Combined Appraisal Wireframes](/images/combined-appraisal-experience-wireframes.png)

### 2. Keep the 20% that mattered

Most ClearCar features were rebranded Max features. But a few were genuinely better: certain appraisal workflows and data views that dealers preferred. I identified those and designed how they'd fold into Max.

**Result:** When dealers tested the prototype, they saw consolidation as an improvement, not a loss.

![Combined Appraisal High-Fidelity Designs](/images/combined-appraisal-experience-high-fidelity-designs.png)

### 3. Show it, don't just recommend it

"We should consolidate" is abstract. I built an interactive prototype of the merged experience, with Max as the base and ClearCar's best parts built in, to answer leadership's real question: *will this break anything?*

**Result:** The roadmap was approved unanimously, and the prototype became the design spec for engineering. Phase one launched in June 2026.

![Combined Appraisal Product Influence](/images/combined-appraisal-experience-product-influence.png)

## What changed

- **For the business:** Engineering capacity is being redirected from duplicate maintenance to larger bets: vehicle intelligence, inventory management, and pricing optimization.
- **For the company:** The audit method (jobs to be done, personas, and journeys) is now used to find other consolidation opportunities.
- **For me:** It made me the cross-platform strategist. I now own or support 8+ product lines.

## What I took away

Sometimes the most strategic product decision is removing a product, not building one. It's less glamorous, but it's often higher impact.

Now I look across the whole portfolio and ask: *is this truly unique, or have we already solved this problem somewhere else?*
